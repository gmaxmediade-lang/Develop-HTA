## 1. What HTA actually is (plain terms both a MoH official and a hospital director respect)

**Definition (use a version of this on the site, plainly):**
HTA is a multidisciplinary, systematic, transparent process that evaluates the properties and effects of a health technology, summarising the medical, social, economic and ethical issues around its use, to inform safe, effective, patient-focused health policy that achieves best value. A "health technology" is any intervention: a test, device, medicine, vaccine, procedure, or program, at any point in its lifecycle (pre-market, regulatory approval, post-market, disinvestment).
Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_technology_assessment ; https://www.definitivehc.com/resources/glossary/health-technology-assessment-hta

**How HTA is USED (the decision it feeds) — this is the part both audiences care about:**
- HTA results inform **coverage and reimbursement** decisions by insurers and national health systems, **clinical guidelines**, **regulatory decisions**, and **pricing negotiations / market access** taken by regulators and payers. For reimbursement it combines a benefit-harm assessment with an economic evaluation. Source: https://www.definitivehc.com/resources/glossary/health-technology-assessment-hta ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_technology_assessment
- In LMICs specifically, HTA is a **priority-setting** tool: it helps policymakers spend limited health budgets more efficiently and equitably, and is central to designing **health benefits packages**, clinical guidelines, and service improvement on the road to **Universal Health Coverage (UHC)**. Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5700536/ ; https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/23288604.2024.2330112

**Plain-language framing that works for both audiences:**
"HTA answers a practical question every health budget faces: of the technologies we *could* buy, which ones actually deliver enough health for the money to be worth funding, and for whom? It turns evidence into a defensible coverage, reimbursement or procurement decision." (synthesised from the two sources above — safe to paraphrase, do not quote as if a source said it verbatim).

**The three concrete decision verbs to put on the site:** coverage, reimbursement, procurement (plus priority-setting / benefits-package design in the LMIC context). All sourced above.

## 2. The African / LMIC HTA context (why Develop-HTA exists — the real, sourced problem)

This is the substance that makes the site credible instead of generic. Every point is documented in peer-reviewed literature:

- **Most Sub-Saharan African Ministries of Health have no formally established HTA agency** to coordinate the process; decision-making is fragmented across government tiers and within the MoH. Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5700536/ ; https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7309411/
- **Awareness of HTA remains low and activities are uncoordinated and often disconnected from policy**; further training and skills development is needed. Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34233710/
- **Local data to support HTA is mostly inadequate/limited**; there is a stated need for training in research methodology and data gathering. Source: https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7309411/
- **HTA is increasingly recognised as a useful policy tool in LMICs** where evidence is needed to guide UHC. Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5700536/
- **Capacity building works even in resource-limited countries** — a structured HTA capacity-building program can be developed and implemented (Jordan example); Ethiopia is actively institutionalizing HTA. Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5749126/ ; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11570159/
- The "usable HTA tools where no formal HTA exists" language in the brief is **accurate and defensible** — it maps directly onto the documented gap. Keep it.

This gives Develop-HTA an honest, evidence-backed reason to exist that it can cite. Recommend a Knowledge-Hub page that links these very papers — that IS the credibility (see section 4).

## 3. Reference organizations in this exact space + how their DIGITAL PRESENCE signals credibility

These are the orgs Develop-HTA is implicitly benchmarked against. Note: most are large; Develop-HTA is a small mission-driven institute (see class note in section 7). Study their *credibility patterns*, not their scale.

**iDSI — international Decision Support Initiative** (closest analogue to Develop-HTA's mission)
- What it is: a collaborative platform founded 2013 by NICE (UK), HITAP (Thailand) and the Center for Global Development, catalyzing evidence-informed priority-setting in LMICs. Core partners include NICE International, HITAP, CGD, and **PRICELESS SA as the sub-Saharan Africa regional hub**. Source: https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/23288604.2024.2330112
- Concrete impact framing they use: "supported eight countries in developing HTA committees, agencies and processes (India, China, Ghana, Tanzania, Vietnam, Bhutan, Philippines, Indonesia)... reviewing 50-60 technologies/year for populations over three billion." Source: https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/23288604.2024.2330112
- Digital-credibility lesson: named partner institutions, a specific country list, and specific throughput numbers — never vague. Tagline "Better decisions for better health." Source: https://idsihealth.wordpress.com/

**HTAi — Health Technology Assessment international** (the founders' own network — see section 6)
- The global scientific/professional society for everyone who produces or uses HTA; **members from 60+ countries, six continents.** Source: https://htai.org/ ; https://htai.eventsair.com/htai-2026-istanbul-annual-meeting/about-htai
- Runs **Interest Groups** (50-300 members each, ~60% of the society), including the **HTA in Developing Countries Interest Group** — the exact group where Marina and Jani met. Source: https://htai.org/interest-groups/ ; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12303693/
- Digital-credibility lesson: HTAi membership is a *nameable, verifiable affiliation* the founders can cite. It is real and checkable.

**WHO** (the standards backbone the brief already points to)
- The **WHO Compendium of Innovative Health Technologies for Low-Resource Settings** provides a neutral platform for technologies likely suitable for less-resourced settings; entries undergo evidence-based assessment (clinical, WHO technical-spec comparison, regulatory, technology-evidence, engineering management). Source: https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240049505 ; https://iris.who.int/server/api/core/bitstreams/1dcaeb34-770d-4475-85e7-a7436eca8a03/content
- **WHO Prequalification** and WHO HTA-for-medical-devices work are the global "safe, effective" reference standard Develop-HTA localises. Source: https://extranet.who.int/prequal/ ; https://cdn.who.int/media/docs/default-source/medical-devices/health-technology-assessment/hta-for-medical-devices-2024-v1.0-for-consultation.pdf
- Digital-credibility lesson: WHO frames everything as **neutral, evidence-based, methodology-first**. Develop-HTA's "global standards, local application" line should visibly link to WHO frameworks. This is the single strongest external anchor available to a pre-registration org.

**PATH** (large med-tech-for-Africa NGO — tone/impact model)
- Advances access to high-quality, safe, effective medical products for UHC; builds capacity, strengthens innovation ecosystems, speeds product delivery. Concrete examples: Umbiflow low-cost Doppler ultrasound reducing stillbirths; shaping South Africa's Medical Technology Masterplan. Source: https://www.path.org/where-we-work/africa/south-africa/ ; https://www.path.org/our-impact/media-center/path-commends-south-africa-on-the-launch-of-its-medical-technology-masterplan/
- Digital-credibility lesson: PATH tells **specific product + specific country + specific outcome** stories under an "Our Impact" hub. That is the template for Develop-HTA's future "Success Stories" — but Develop-HTA has none yet, so those slots stay honestly empty (section 5).

**Africa CDC** (African-Union-owned; sovereignty/local-leadership tone model)
- African Union public-health agency; strengthens Africa's public-health institutions and systems to prevent/detect/respond based on evidence-based policies; frames a "New Africa Public Health Order" around **health security and sovereignty**. Source: https://africacdc.org/about-us/our-mission/ ; https://africacdc.org/news-item/africa-cdc-unveils-a-new-vision-for-health-security-and-sovereignty-across-the-continent/
- Digital-credibility lesson: the credible African-facing frame is **sovereignty, systems strengthening, evidence** — African institutions leading, not being rescued. Mirror this language.

**Amref Health Africa** (Africa-led NGO tone model)
- Africa-based/Africa-led; works on One Health, AMR, systems strengthening; frames Africa securing "its own health future." Source: https://newsroom.amref.org/blog/2025/12/from-crisis-to-opportunity-secure-africas-health-future-now/

**Center for Global Development (CGD)** (evidence/value-for-money think-tank model)
- Co-founder of iDSI; publishes rigorous analyses on **value for money, evidence-informed locally-led prioritization, domestic-first financing.** Source: https://www.cgdev.org/blog/international-decision-support-initiative-scaling-up-means-better-decisions-better-health ; https://www.cgdev.org/publication/new-compact-financing-health-services-opportunities-gavi-and-partner-countries
- Digital-credibility lesson: think-tank credibility = **publications + named authors + methodology**, not photography. This is the most attainable model for a 2-person institute with no field photos yet.

**Gavi** (advocacy/impact-narrative model)
- Relies on narratives of **highly cost-effective services** for advocacy/fundraising; runs a Health Systems Strengthening window. Source: https://www.cgdev.org/sites/default/files/More-Health-for-the-Money.pdf ; https://www.gavi.org/vaccineswork/how-evidence-driven-policy-analysis-and-advocacy-strengthens-immunisation-systems

**Consolidated digital-credibility pattern across all of them:** neutral/evidence-first voice; named institutions and named people; specific country lists; methodology visible; impact told as specific-outcome-in-specific-place; sovereignty/local-leadership framing for African audiences. None lead with stock "globe" imagery — that is exactly the amateur signal to avoid (matches the brief's instruction to replace the AI-globe draft).

## 4. Credibility signals a health-policy non-profit MUST have on its site

From nonprofit-transparency and global-health-trust literature. Ranked by relevance to Develop-HTA:

1. **Named people with real credentials.** Show the people behind the org — bios in About/Team, credentials and expertise, a touch of humanity. For Develop-HTA: Marina (MD) + Jani (biomedical engineer) + their HTAi Developing-Countries Interest-Group origin. This is the #1 trust lever for a small org. Source: https://nonprofitwebsiteinsider.com/10-guidelines-for-nonprofit-website-credibility/ ; https://www.bluehillsdigital.com/articles/nonprofit-website-build-trust-credibility/
2. **Methodology / evidence visible alongside claims.** The most-trusted global-health orgs (GiveWell, Against Malaria Foundation, GiveDirectly cited as exemplars) show *methodology next to results*; impact = specific numbers tied to specific outcomes in specific geographies and time periods. Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8735731/ ; https://www.givewell.org/about/givewell-overview
3. **Third-party verification / cross-references.** Link out to third-party support (WHO frameworks, peer-reviewed papers) for claims you present; cross-references make people trust the information more even if they don't click. **This is Develop-HTA's cheapest, strongest move** — the Knowledge Hub linking WHO + the SSA-HTA papers in section 2. Source: https://nonprofitwebsiteinsider.com/10-guidelines-for-nonprofit-website-credibility/
4. **Governance / registration / financial transparency.** Publishing org documents and registration signals "nothing to hide"; Charity Navigator treats a website + IRS-990-type filings as accountability metrics. Develop-HTA is **pre-registration**, so it cannot show these yet — handle honestly (section 5). Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8735731/ ; https://www.charitynavigator.org/ein/043481920
5. **A focused homepage that proves the org is active and credible before asking for anything** — one clear message, 1-2 CTAs, proof of credibility; nonprofits must earn trust before asking for action (a higher trust bar than commercial sites). Source: https://www.wandr.studio/blog/nonprofit-website-design-the-complete-2026-guide ; https://weareloop.com/best-nonprofit-websites/
6. **Partner logos / named affiliations** — powerful, but only real ones. Develop-HTA can honestly cite the **HTAi affiliation** (verifiable). Do NOT display WHO/PATH/etc. logos as if they were partners — they are *reference standards*, not partners. (Inference from the transparency literature above + brief's no-fabrication constraint.)

## 5. How to present a 2-person, pre-registration org honestly (no fabricated proof)

The hard constraint: no client proof, no field photos, entities not yet registered. The literature is clear you can still be credible:

- **Lead with mission clarity + credentialed founders + linked evidence, not with fake impact.** A homepage should lead with a clear message and *proof the org is active and credible*, not everything at once. Founders' credentials + real cited frameworks ARE that proof. Source: https://www.wandr.studio/blog/nonprofit-website-design-the-complete-2026-guide ; https://nonprofitwebsiteinsider.com/10-guidelines-for-nonprofit-website-credibility/
- **Honest "coming soon" slots are legitimate.** Solo/founding-stage nonprofits routinely launch lean; the credibility comes from what you *do* show being true and verifiable, not from volume. Empty "Success Stories" / "Impact" sections should read as *forthcoming*, dated, and framed as an invitation to partner — never as achieved results. Source: https://nonprofitwebsiteinsider.com/10-guidelines-for-nonprofit-website-credibility/ (verification-through-cross-reference principle)
- **Practical honesty rules for this build (recommendation, synthesised):**
  - Impact numbers: NONE until real. Replace with the *sourced problem* (section 2 stats about the SSA HTA gap) — those are real, cited, and make the case without claiming Develop-HTA's own results.
  - Team: real names, real credentials (MD, biomedical engineer), real HTAi origin story. No invented advisory board.
  - Partners: only HTAi affiliation (verifiable). WHO/PATH/iDSI shown as "frameworks we build on / standards we localise," clearly labelled as references, never as endorsements.
  - Registration/governance: a plain, honest "Develop-HTA is being established as a European-based non-profit with a Sub-Saharan consultancy arm" line beats silence and beats over-claiming a registered charity status it doesn't have.
  - Success Stories / testimonials: visible "coming soon / in development" states, dated, framed as "we are seeking our first partner ministries and hospitals."
- **The founders' credibility rests on:** (a) real professional credentials — a medical doctor + a biomedical engineer is a genuinely well-matched HTA pairing (clinical + device/engineering, which mirrors WHO's own med-device HTA competencies: clinical + engineering-management assessment, section 3 WHO source); (b) the **HTAi Developing-Countries Interest-Group** network — a real, checkable global professional affiliation. Present both plainly; do not inflate into institutional heft they don't have yet.

## 6. Tone & language conventions for this audience (MoH, hospitals, donors, device makers across SSA)

The audience is sophisticated and politically alert. Get the register wrong and you lose ministries instantly.

**DO:**
- **Sober, evidence-first, neutral voice** — mirror WHO's "neutral platform / evidence-based" register. Source: https://iris.who.int/server/api/core/bitstreams/1dcaeb34-770d-4475-85e7-a7436eca8a03/content
- **"Global standards, local application"** framing — this is exactly right and is the documented value proposition (global frameworks are only useful when localised to local data/context). Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5700536/
- **Sovereignty / local-leadership / systems-strengthening** language — Africa CDC's own frame is "health security and sovereignty," African institutions leading. Position Develop-HTA as a *bridge and capacity-builder*, with African ministries and experts as the decision-makers. Source: https://africacdc.org/news-item/africa-cdc-unveils-a-new-vision-for-health-security-and-sovereignty-across-the-continent/
- **Equity + Universal Health Coverage** language — the field's shared goal; HTA as the tool to spend limited budgets efficiently and equitably. Source: https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/23288604.2024.2330112
- **Local ownership / capacity toward leadership** — the decolonising-global-health literature explicitly calls for LMIC-led work with external actors in *peripheral support* roles, and enhancing local capacity toward ownership. Frame Develop-HTA's consultancy arm as building local capacity, not importing expertise. Source: https://www.thelancet.com/journals/langlo/article/PIIS2214-109X(21)00317-X/fulltext ; https://journals.plos.org/globalpublichealth/article?id=10.1371/journal.pgph.0003141

**DON'T (tone TRAPS — flag hard):**
- **White-savior framing / "empower Africa" as rescue.** Decolonising-global-health scholarship explicitly names eradicating "White savior" complexes and the over-representation of HIC actors leading. A European-founded org working in Africa must be *especially* careful: position as partner/bridge/capacity-builder, with local actors as leaders — never "we empower/save the Sub-Saharan system." The brief's own draft subline "We empower the Sub-Saharan healthcare system" leans into this trap and should be softened toward partnership/bridging language. Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7409954/ ; https://www.thelancet.com/journals/langlo/article/PIIS2214-109X(21)00317-X/fulltext
- **Poverty-porn / deficit imagery** — no distressed-patient, crumbling-clinic, "helpless Africa" photography. This reproduces the colonial gaze the audience is actively critiquing. Use clean clinical/data/technology imagery and (later) dignified professional-in-context photography.
- **Paternalism** — avoid language implying African institutions lack the sophistication to decide; they lack *tooling and coordination* (the real, sourced gap), not intelligence. Source: https://journals.plos.org/globalpublichealth/article?id=10.1371/journal.pgph.0003141
- **Over-claiming / vague grandeur** — donors and ministries trust specificity and methodology, not adjectives. No "world-leading," no invented scale. Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8735731/
- **Homogenising "Africa"** — "Sub-Saharan Africa" is 40+ countries with different health systems; the credible orgs name specific countries (iDSI's country list, PATH's South Africa work). Where Develop-HTA can't yet name a country, speak to "ministries and hospitals across Sub-Saharan Africa" rather than implying continent-wide operations it doesn't have.

**Net recommended voice:** the register of a serious health-policy institute (CGD / iDSI / WHO) — calm, precise, evidence-linked, partnership-framed — NOT a charity-appeal or a glossy consultancy pitch.

## 7. Class / benchmark note (do not build above the org's real weight)

Per the brief's Benchmark Selection constraint: Develop-HTA is a **small, mission-driven global-health / health-policy institute** (2 founders, pre-registration, non-profit budget), NOT a large foundation. The right *craft* benchmark for digital presence is the **think-tank / policy-institute** pattern (CGD, iDSI, NICE International) — publications, named experts, methodology, clean clinical design, cited frameworks — which is achievable with **no field photography and no client proof**. It is explicitly NOT the PATH/Gavi/Africa-CDC pattern, which depends on large operations, field photography and impact numbers Develop-HTA does not have. Building in the think-tank class is both honest and attainable. Source (patterns): https://www.cgdev.org/topics/priority-setting ; https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/23288604.2024.2330112 ; https://www.givewell.org/about/givewell-overview

- **Home:** one clear message (bridge global HTA standards to local application for safe, effective, sustainable technology in SSA), 1-2 CTAs, immediate proof-of-credibility (founders + HTAi + cited WHO frameworks). No AI globe. Source: https://www.wandr.studio/blog/nonprofit-website-design-the-complete-2026-guide

- **About / Our Story:** real founders, real credentials, real HTAi-Developing-Countries origin; the hybrid model (European non-profit core + SSA consultancy arm) stated plainly; honest pre-registration status. Source: https://nonprofitwebsiteinsider.com/10-guidelines-for-nonprofit-website-credibility/
- **Knowledge Hub = the credibility engine.** Curate + link the real WHO frameworks (Compendium, Prequalification, HTA-for-medical-devices) and the SSA-HTA literature (section 2). Cross-referencing verifiable sources is the single strongest, cheapest trust signal available pre-launch. "Success Stories" and "Experts network" ship as honest, dated "coming soon / in development" states. Source: https://nonprofitwebsiteinsider.com/10-guidelines-for-nonprofit-website-credibility/ ; https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240049505
- **Impact:** replace any invented numbers with the *sourced problem* (real, cited SSA HTA-gap statistics) until Develop-HTA has its own results. Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34233710/ ; https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7309411/
- **Tone guardrail for all copy:** partnership/bridge/local-leadership, evidence-first, no savior language, no poverty imagery, name specifics not adjectives. Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7409954/
