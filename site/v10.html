How an assessment works

From an open question to a decision you can act on.

01

Question

A ministry, hospital or funder brings a real decision: adopt this medicine, device or diagnostic, or not. We turn it into one precise, answerable question.

02

Evidence

We gather the clinical trials, real-world data and full costs, then quality-check every source against international HTA standards. The answer rests on what is proven, not what is marketed.

03

Appraisal

Benefit, safety and cost are weighed together for the local context: disease burden, infrastructure and budget. The result fits the system in front of us, not a European average.

04

Recommendation

A clear recommendation with its reasoning shown: adopt, negotiate or decline. A decision you can defend to a board, a ministry or the public.

Questions

The things people ask us first.

What is Health Technology Assessment?

A structured way of judging whether a medicine, device or diagnostic works, is safe, and is worth its cost, before a health system commits to it.

Are you a non-profit or a consultancy?

Both, by design. A European non-profit core holds the mission and methods; a Sub-Saharan consultancy arm delivers assessments. The model is being formalised now.

Who do you work with?

Health ministries, hospitals, funders and manufacturers who need an independent read on whether a technology fits a given system.

Where are you active?

Our focus is Sub-Saharan Africa, with methods anchored in European and global HTA practice. We are pre-launch and building our first partnerships.

How is an assessment paid for?

Through a mix of philanthropic and institutional support for the non-profit core, and fees from the parties who commission a specific assessment. The exact model is being formalised as we register, and any cost is agreed openly before work begins.

What evidence does an assessment draw on?

Clinical trial and safety data on the technology itself, local cost, budget and disease burden figures, and existing assessments from other health systems. We adapt that global evidence to the realities of the setting we are advising, rather than assuming a European result transfers directly.
