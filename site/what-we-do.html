What we do

Global standards are only as good as their local application. We are a bridge, not just a technical repository, turning evidence into decisions health systems can act on.

The approach

We work evidence first, building on the standards the field already trusts rather than claiming authority of our own.

Every assessment we produce is grounded in established, public sources. We adapt them to the realities of the settings we serve, where budgets, infrastructure, and formal HTA structures differ from where these standards were written. What we build on:

WHO prequalification of health products, the global benchmark for safe, quality-assured medicine and devices.
WHO compendium of innovative health technologies for low-resource settings, 2024.
PATH technical standards for health technologies in the settings we serve.

Four ways we turn evidence into decisions.

Assess and compare technologies

Independent assessments that weigh safety, effectiveness, and value, so a hospital or ministry can compare real alternatives against real budgets before it invests.

  • Structured evidence review against WHO and PATH standards
  • Head-to-head comparison of viable technologies
  • Clear recommendation, with the assumptions made visible

HTA-informed dossiers and modeling

Economic and scenario modeling, plus evidence dossiers that strengthen procurement decisions, grant applications, and conversations with payers.

  • Cost and budget-impact scenarios grounded in local data
  • Evidence dossiers aligned with payer expectations
  • Sensitivity analysis so decisions hold under uncertainty

Capacity building and training

Practical training and structured mentoring that help emerging HTA units and local teams run assessments independently, so the capability stays after the engagement ends.

  • Training modules for teams new to HTA
  • Hands-on mentoring through a first live assessment
  • Reusable templates and methods left with the team

Advisory for coverage and procurement

Structured advisory for coverage, reimbursement, and procurement decisions, usable where a formal HTA process does not yet exist.

  • Decision frameworks for coverage and reimbursement
  • Procurement criteria that reward value, not just price
  • Support tailored to institutions building HTA from scratch
